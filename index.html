Enrollment

Insurance enrollment deadline

As a new legislator, you have 30 days from November 3, 2026 to enroll yourself and your dependents in state insurance. The deadline is December 2, 2026. If you elect coverage within the 30 days, the effective date is 1/1/2027.

If you miss the 30-day deadline, you cannot enroll yourself or your dependents until the next annual enrollment period. Annual enrollment changes take effect January 1 of the following year.

Dependent documentation

You must submit dependent verification documents to add eligible dependents. The list is in the Medical section under Dependent eligibility.

If you have trouble getting the documents, you get an additional 10 days to submit them. You must still submit your enrollment to Benefits Administration within the 30 days to receive the extra 10.

CDHP state health savings account (HSA) contribution

For people who enroll in the CDHP: $500 for employee-only coverage and $1,000 for all other coverage levels.

For PPO plans, no single family member is subject to a deductible or out-of-pocket maximum greater than the "employee only" amount. Once two or more family members (depending on premium level) have met the total deductible and/or out-of-pocket maximum, it is met for all covered family members. For the CDHP, the deductible and out-of-pocket maximum can be met by one or more persons but must be met in full before it is considered satisfied.

  • [1] Subject to maximum allowable charge (MAC), the most a plan will pay for a covered service or medication.   • [2] Residential treatment, partial hospitalization/day treatment and intensive outpatient therapy are treated as "inpatient" for cost-sharing. Dental

Partners for Health 2027 dental insurance

Two options are available: a dental health maintenance organization (DHMO), a prepaid plan administered by Cigna, and a dental preferred provider organization (DPPO) administered by MetLife.

Cigna DHMO

  • No deductible, no annual maximum and no waiting periods.
  • You pay copays listed in the Patient Charge Schedule, plus lab fees for certain services.
  • Teledentistry is $0 copay with no frequency limit.
  • Exams and cleanings: oral evaluations are limited to a combined 4 per 12 consecutive months (periodic, comprehensive oral, and comprehensive periodontal evaluations). Cleanings: 2 per calendar year, and you can add 2 more per year at a $45 copay.
  • Orthodontics: $3,360 maximum ($140 x 24 months) for the treatment fee only. You pay the full charge after 24 months. No age limit.
  • Network: you must select and use a Cigna network general dentist from the DHMO list for the state dental plan and tell Cigna your choice. You may select a network pediatric dentist for a child under 13, who is considered a specialist.
  • Check the network for your location before enrolling. You may be able to cancel if there are no network general dentists within 25 driving miles of your home.
  • Find providers: hcpdirectory.cigna.com/web/public/consumer/directory/search?consumerCode=HDC060

MetLife DPPO

  • Deductible: in-network $50 single, $150 family per plan year. Out-of-network $100 single, $300 family per plan year.
  • Plan maximum: $1,500 benefit per person per calendar year.
  • You pay the deductible and coinsurance. The provider negotiated fee is the highest amount MetLife DPPO in-network providers can charge members or the plan for a procedure.
  • In-network coinsurance: diagnostic and preventive 0%; basic services 20%; major services 50%; orthodontic services 50%.
  • Exams and cleanings: 2 routine office exams and 2 problem-focused exams (office or teledentistry) per calendar year.
  • Orthodontics: $1,500 lifetime plan benefit maximum per person.
  • No waiting periods.
  • Network: you can use any dentist, but you save money and get maximum benefits with an in-network MetLife PDP Plus provider. Find providers at metlife.com/StateOfTN.

Details: tn.gov/partnersforhealth/other-benefits/dental. Review member handbooks and certificates of coverage at tn.gov/partnersforhealth/publications/publications. Request pre-treatment estimates to understand costs before service.

How the 5x and 7x limits fit together: without health questions, you can elect up to 5x your base annual salary (the guaranteed issue amount). Above that, up to 7x salary or $500,000, whichever is less, you must answer health questions.

Coverage available with no health questions

"Initial eligibility" is the first time a person is eligible for coverage. For you, that is when you are hired and become eligible for benefits. For your spouse, it is when you become eligible or within 30 days of a new marriage.

Within 30 days of initial eligibility

  • Voluntary employee term life: elect in $5,000 increments, not to exceed $500,000 or five times base annual salary, whichever is less
  • Voluntary spouse term life: elect $5,000
  • Voluntary child term life rider: elect $5,000 or $10,000
  • Employee voluntary AD&D: $50,000, $60,000, $100,000, $250,000 or $500,000
  • Employee + dependents voluntary AD&D: spouse without children 60%, spouse with children 40%, each child 10%

During each annual enrollment

  • Voluntary employee term life: increase existing coverage by $5,000, not to exceed $500,000 or five times base annual salary, whichever is less
  • Voluntary child term life rider: elect $5,000 or $10,000
  • Employee voluntary AD&D: $50,000, $60,000, $100,000, $250,000 or $500,000
  • Employee + dependents voluntary AD&D: spouse without children 60%, spouse with children 40%, each child 10%

Adding a child term rider to voluntary term life, and enrolling in voluntary AD&D, never requires health questions when elected during any of these events.

Coverage that requires health questions

You answer questions about your health history, plus height and weight.

Within 30 days of initial eligibility

  • Apply for voluntary employee term life above the guaranteed issue amount of five times your annual base salary, up to $500,000
  • Apply for voluntary spouse term life above the guaranteed issue amount of $5,000, up to plan maximums

During each annual enrollment

  • Apply for the first time for voluntary employee term life
  • Increase existing voluntary employee term life above $5,000, up to plan maximums
  • Apply for the first time for voluntary spouse term life
  • Increase existing voluntary spouse term life, up to plan maximums

Medical flexible spending account (FSA)

Contribute pretax dollars to pay for health care expenses. Each $1 you contribute reduces your taxable income by $1.

Eligible expenses include

  • Copays for office visits
  • Deductibles and coinsurance
  • Dental and orthodontic services
  • Eye exams, glasses and contact lenses
  • First-aid kits and supplies
  • Flu shots and vaccinations
  • Over-the-counter medications
  • Prescription drugs

Good to know

  • You do not qualify for a medical FSA if you are enrolled in the consumer-driven health plan with a health savings account.
  • The TASC debit card is the preferred way to pay for eligible expenses. It lets you pay and verify most expenses at the point of purchase, with no reimbursement wait.
  • Full eligible-expense lists: IRS Publications 502 and 969 at irs.gov.
  • Estimate your savings with the FSA Calculator at tasconline.com/fsa-calculator.

Health savings account (HSA)

An HSA works with your consumer-driven health plan. Use it to pay medical expenses your CDHP does not cover, such as office visit copays, prescription drugs, OTC medications, and flu shots and vaccinations. Each $1 you contribute reduces your taxable income by $1.

A triple tax advantage

  1. Contributions into an HSA are pretax.
  2. Earned interest on investment funds is tax free.
  3. Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax free.

You own the HSA

The account and its funds stay with you, even if you change jobs, and it stays active if you are no longer covered by a CDHP. You can no longer contribute if you are not covered by a CDHP. HSA funds never expire and can be used for expenses incurred in any year beyond your enrollment in the TASC HSA plan.

Limited purpose FSA (LPFSA)

A limited purpose FSA can only be used for eligible vision and dental expenses. Unlike a medical FSA, you can hold it at the same time as an HSA. When you or your spouse enroll in an HSA plan, you can no longer participate in a full medical FSA, but you can enroll in an LPFSA through your employer-sponsored plan. Relying on your LPFSA for vision or dental costs leaves more in your HSA to grow.

Vision expenses

  • Eye exams
  • Prescription glasses and contact lenses
  • Contact lens solution
  • Laser eye surgery, LASIK

Dental expenses

  • Orthodontia (braces, Invisalign)
  • Cleanings
  • Crowns
  • Fillings

An LPFSA is subject to the same IRS plan limits as a medical FSA. Eligible expenses: IRS Publication 502 at irs.gov.

Dependent care benefits

The dependent care FSA lets you pay with pretax dollars for care of your child, disabled spouse, elderly parent or other dependent so you (or your spouse) can work, look for work or attend school full time.

Eligible expenses must meet these criteria

  • Work-related. The care must be necessary for you (or your spouse) to work, look for work or attend school full time, or because you are physically unable to care for your children.
  • Limit. Effective Jan. 1, 2026, reimbursement in a calendar year cannot exceed $7,500 ($3,750 for a married individual filing a separate return). It may be less if your or your spouse's earned income is under $7,500.
  • Qualifying person. A dependent child who was younger than 13 when care was provided and for whom a tax exemption can be claimed; a spouse who was physically or mentally unable to care for themselves and lived with you more than half the year; or a dependent who was physically or mentally unable to care for themselves, for whom an exemption can be claimed, and who lived with you more than half the year.

Special rules for children of divorced or separated parents

Even if you cannot claim your child as a dependent, the child is your qualifying person if all of these are true:

  • The child was younger than 13 or was not physically or mentally able to care for themselves.
  • One or both parents provided more than half of the child's support for the year and are divorced, legally separated or lived apart at all times during the last six months of the calendar year.
  • One or both parents had custody of the child for more than half of the year.
  • You were the custodial parent, meaning the parent with custody for the greater part of the year. If the child spent equal nights with both parents, the parent with the higher adjusted gross income is the custodial parent.

A non-custodial parent entitled to claim the child as a tax dependent cannot treat the child as a qualifying individual for the dependent care FSA, even if financially responsible for the care. Only the custodial parent may qualify for a taxable year.
